Grailcard

Technical Implementation & Competitive Analysis
Version 0.1 (Milestone 1 + Identification)  ·  August 18, 2026  ·  Internal report

1. Executive Summary

Grailcard is a trading-card pre-grading system built on a single thesis: in a market where every competitor advertises unfalsifiable accuracy percentages, the defensible position is demonstrated trustworthiness — refuse bad input instead of guessing from it, output measurements instead of opinions, attach a confidence figure to every claim, and say plainly when something cannot be measured.

This document describes what has been implemented, how each stage works, the measured accuracy of the system against ground truth, and a same-image, head-to-head comparison against CardGrader.AI — the most instructive result in this report. On identical input, Grailcard produced a small number of verifiable claims (correct identification, a centering measurement with a disclosed low-confidence flag, and a market price consistent with live exchange data), while CardGrader.AI produced a detailed, authoritative-looking report containing a misidentification worth a ~300× valuation error, sub-grades for physical defects on an image of a card that does not physically exist, and internally contradictory price estimates.

2. Design Thesis and Market Context

The AI pre-grading market (PreGradeCards, CardGrade.io, CardMintAI, TCGrader, ZeroPop, CardGrading.app, SnapGradeAI, CardGrader.AI, and others) is crowded and priced to the floor: entry plans near $4.99/month, per-card pricing around $0.25, free scans used for acquisition. Vendors self-report 87–95% accuracy on their own test sets; independent observation puts photo-based AI grading at roughly 70–85% agreement with PSA, usually "within one grade" — a tolerance that is commercially meaningless at the top of the scale, where the difference between a 9 and a 10 is the entire economic question.

Grailcard's differentiation is structural, not incremental:

  • Refuse bad input. A quality gate rejects blurry, glared, small, or low-resolution photos with a specific retry instruction, at no charge — instead of returning a number from data that cannot support one.
  • Measure, don't opine. Centering is computed geometrically from the printed border and reported as ratios (e.g. 52/48), with the measured border lines drawn on the image so the user can see exactly where the measurement was taken.
  • Report uncertainty. Every measurement carries a confidence score derived from signal quality, and it is shown to the user — including when it is low.
  • Honest non-answers. Borderless/full-art cards return "centering not measurable" rather than a fabricated ratio. Claims the system cannot yet make (corners, edges, surface) are absent, not invented.

All computer vision is in-house. No third-party vision API is used in production — a deliberate decision (§7): building on a vendor such as Ximilar (~€0.06–0.10 per graded scan) caps accuracy at the vendor's model, makes the product a reseller of calls every competitor can buy, and forecloses the differentiators above, none of which a generic API exposes. External services are limited to commodity data: the free TCGdex catalog (identification + market prices), fetched server-side with graceful degradation.

4. Pipeline Implementation

4.1 Detection and normalization (detect.py)

The card quad is located with a two-strategy contour search: Canny edges (dilated) first, then an Otsu binarization fallback that still separates a light card from a darker background when heavy blur destroys the edge response — so a blurry photo is found and then rejected for the true reason (blur) rather than misreported as "no card". Candidate contours are filtered by area (2–95% of frame) and aspect ratio (0.55–0.90 short/long, around the 63×88 mm card's 0.716), then perspective-warped to a canonical 750×1050 crop. Landscape shots are auto-rotated.

4.2 Quality gate (quality.py)

Applied most-fundamental-failure-first; every rejection carries a plain-language reason and a concrete retry hint. All thresholds live in a single config file.

4.3 Centering measurement (centering.py)

On the canonical crop the card's outer edge is the image edge, so centering reduces to locating the inner print border on each side. For each side, the mean absolute Sobel gradient is profiled across the central 70% band (corners excluded) within a search window covering the outer 25% of the card; the dominant peak marks the border. Ratios are pure pixel arithmetic. The peak's strength relative to the profile median yields both a measurability verdict (ratio ≥ 3 required — borderless cards honestly fail this) and a confidence score. PSA pass verdicts apply published standards: front ≤60/40 for a 10, ≤65/35 for a 9; back ≤75/25 and ≤90/10 when a back capture is provided. An annotated overlay (the four measured border lines drawn on the card) is returned with every measurement.

4.4 Identification (identify.py + API tcgdex.ts)

RapidOCR (offline ONNX; no per-call cost) reads the warped front. The title is taken from prominent text in the top band, cleaned of OCR artifacts (merged spaces are split on case boundaries; mangled "ex/GX" logo fragments are stripped); the collector number is regex-matched in the bottom band. The API then queries the free TCGdex catalog with progressive seeds and scores candidates by character-bigram Dice similarity, with a bonus when the printed collector number agrees; matches below 0.45 are discarded rather than guessed. Identification intentionally runs even when the grading gate rejects the photo — reading a title needs far less resolution than measuring a border, so a rejected photo still tells the user which card was seen and what it is worth. The UI always shows the raw OCR text and the match percentage.

4.5 Valuation

TCGdex card records carry live TCGplayer (USD low/mid/high/market, per print variant) and Cardmarket (EUR low/trend/30-day average) prices with update timestamps; these are displayed explicitly labeled raw / ungraded, with the price date. The production plan replaces per-request fetching with nightly snapshot ingestion into Postgres (PriceCharting for graded-slab prices, JustTCG for raw condition-specific prices), keeping data costs flat (<$60/month) and independent of scan volume while accumulating proprietary price history.

The vision pipeline is verified by a golden-fixture suite (20 tests, all passing) built on a synthetic generator that renders a card with exactly known border ratios, rotated 8° like a handheld shot, then composited and re-measured through the full pipeline. End-to-end over HTTP (web → API → vision → SQLite → report), a front/back pair measured 59/41 front (true 60/40) and 68/32 back (true 70/30), with the combined PSA verdict correctly applying the looser back standard.

Disclosed limitation — modern thin-border cards. On a modern ME-era card (Mega Slowbro ex) with a very thin silver frame, the border scan locked onto an artwork edge and reported 23/77 where truth is ~50/50. Classic-border cards measure correctly. Per-era border calibration is the top roadmap item; until then the product's own confidence score is the guardrail — thin, ambiguous border peaks produce low confidence, and that figure is always displayed.

6. Case Study: Same Image, Grailcard vs CardGrader.AI

A single test image — the official Base Set Charizard #4 render (Unlimited edition), composited onto a dark background at a slight angle — was submitted to both systems on August 18, 2026. The image is perfectly centered by construction and depicts no physical card at all.

What the comparison actually shows. The failure mode is not "their model is less accurate." It is architectural: a system designed to always deliver a rich answer will fabricate whatever the answer requires — an edition it cannot verify, corner wear that does not exist, a coherent-sounding narrative wrapped around incoherent numbers. A misidentified edition alone turned a ~$850 card into a "$67,000" one. Grailcard's design makes fewer claims, and each is traceable: the OCR text is shown, the measured border lines are drawn on the image, the confidence is printed, and the capabilities it lacks are simply absent from the report.
Grailcard's own errors in this test, disclosed. (1) The T/B measurement of 41/59 on a perfectly centered render is ~9pp off — the composite's JPEG compression and the render's border profile produced weak gradient peaks, and the system flagged exactly that by reporting 27% confidence; the number should be read accordingly. (2) More fundamentally, both systems analyzed a picture of a digital image as though it were a physical card. Grailcard should detect and refuse screen photos, prints, and renders (no print grain, no paper texture); this authenticity gate is on the roadmap and is precisely the kind of refusal the product's thesis demands.

Grailcard's cost structure against this field: all vision in-house at ~$0.003 marginal cost per scan (compute + storage), identification and valuation on free catalog data, and a planned flat <$60/month for premium price feeds — versus €0.06–0.10/scan perpetual COGS and a shared accuracy ceiling for every wrapper built on the same vendor API.

8. Current Limitations

  • Centering only. Corners, edges, and surface are not yet analyzed; the product makes no claims about them. (Corner-macro capture and trained per-criterion models are Phases 3–4.)
  • Modern thin-border cards can mislead the border scan (§5); low confidence is the current guardrail, per-era calibration the fix.
  • No authenticity / digital-source detection. A high-quality render or screen photo passes the gate (§6). Planned: texture/print-grain analysis to refuse non-physical sources.
  • Valuation is raw-market only. Graded-slab prices, population reports, and the submit-or-not expected-value calculation await the paid data integrations.
  • Identification is OCR-driven. Extremely low-resolution or non-Latin cards can fail to match; the embedding-based visual index (DINOv2 + pgvector) is the Phase 2 upgrade.

9. Roadmap

  1. Per-era border calibration keyed off identification (fixes modern thin-border centering).
  2. Authenticity gate: refuse renders, screen photos, and prints.
  3. Live guided capture in-browser (corner brackets with real-time quality checks before the shot).
  4. Embedding-based identification index; corner-macro captures; corner/edge analyzers.
  5. Graded-price data + population reports → grade-band probabilities and submission ROI.
  6. Public prediction-vs-actual dashboard verified against PSA cert numbers — the falsifiable accuracy claim no competitor currently publishes.
