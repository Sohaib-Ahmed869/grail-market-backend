Valuation accuracy: measured against Collectr

Today's work addressed six defects raised from client testing. The system no longer grades cards; it identifies the printing, reads the grading label, and prices that exact grade from completed sales. Accuracy is stated against Collectr as the reference, on the cards where a direct comparison exists.

Issues raised by the client, and what each turned out to be

ReportedActual causeResolution
Wrong card identified The grading label was read but discarded. Where the label's grade digit was unreadable, the whole label was thrown away, dropping the card to fuzzy name matching — a Legendary Collection Charizard resolved to Dragon Frontiers, and a Deoxys ex to POP Series 4. Failing to read a grade is no longer treated as evidence the card is ungraded. The label's set and number stay available for identification, and every plausible label line is scored against the catalogue.
Beckett shown as PSA When the company logo failed to OCR, the code guessed, and the guess defaulted to PSA. Detection now keys on markers Beckett prints and PSA never does — the four subgrade captions and the “+” suffix. Where nothing indicates a company it reports unknown instead of inventing one.
Grading wildly wrong
51 marks on a clean card		 The v0 heuristics were not fit to move money, and their output was also being applied as a price multiplier. Grading removed entirely — see below.
PSA 9 priced at ~$150 Two faults stacked: the wrong card, and a raw price quoted for a card known to be slabbed. A raw price is never quoted for a slabbed card. Where the grade cannot be read the price is withheld and the state is named.
Raw card under-valued
$84 shown as $21		 Our own grade of 2.5 was applied as a 0.25 multiplier against the market price. Condition adjustment removed with the grade that produced it. A raw card is quoted at the raw market price.
Prices showing $NaN Deploy skew — the browser held an older bundle expecting a plain number while the API had moved to a richer object. Tiles accept either shape. A stale price is recoverable; a price reading NaN is not.

The two changes that produced most of the accuracy gain

Reading the provider's filtered price

The price source publishes two figures per grade: a raw median and a filtered, recency-weighted price. We were reading the raw one. On the Deoxys, the sample ran from $80.63 to $1,882.55 for the same card at the same grade — mistitled and damaged listings sitting in the median at full weight. The filtered figure is $1,868.84. Across the tested set the two differ by −51% to +107%.

Reading the grade word, not the digit

PSA prints the same fact twice, and the two are not equally legible. OCR read the isolated 9 on a MINT label as a 6, and sometimes dropped it entirely. PSA's scale is published and fixed — MINT is always 9 — so the multi-character word now decides and the digit only confirms.

Removed

  • Our condition grading, on every card — slabbed and raw
  • Sub-grades for corners, edges and surface
  • Surface-mark detection and the annotated detection view
  • Centering measurement and its PSA pass/fail verdict
  • The condition multiplier that discounted market prices
  • Grade / don't-grade recommendations and net-profit tables
  • Provisional grades previously returned on rejected photos

A card already certified by PSA or Beckett does not need our opinion, and for a raw card the heuristics were not accurate enough to influence a price.

Added

  • A grade tuple — company, grade, qualifier, label variant, sub-grades, tier — replacing a single number
  • 14 grading companies, including Beckett half-grades, BVG, BCCG, Black Label, CGC's two tens and SGC's legacy 100-point scale
  • Guards against “PSA 10 candidate”, multi-card lots and Raw Card Review being read as certifications
  • Prices per grading company, with an honest empty state where we hold no data for that company
  • Sample size and confidence on every figure; a single-sale price is flagged as an anecdote
  • Rejection of unreadable slab photos rather than guessing
  • Paste-a-screenshot capture
